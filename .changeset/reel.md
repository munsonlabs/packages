'@munsonlabs/reel': minor
---

Experimental first cut of reel: `createClip()` trims a video, crops it to 9:16 (or any aspect) and exports an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio entirely in the browser with WebCodecs and Mediabunny. AAC audio is copied without re-encoding and other audio is encoded as AAC; where this browser has no AAC encoder (Firefox), a source with other audio gives a silent clip and `onWarning({ reason: 'audio-unavailable', target: 'audio', message })` (`console.warn` by default). An abort that lands while the file is being finalised still rejects with the signal's reason. Sources are a `Blob`, a URL or a `<video>`. `canClip()` reads only a source's header and answers what would happen to the audio (`plan.audio`: `'copy'`, `'encode'`, `'none'` or `'unavailable'`) or, as a stable `reason` code, why it cannot be clipped (an embed page, DRM, MediaSource, a MediaStream, DASH, a cross-origin file without CORS, an unreadable container, an undecodable video, no H.264 encoder, no WebCodecs); `createClip` rejects with a `ClipError` carrying the same code. `support()` reports the H.264 encoder and decoder and the AAC encoder this browser has.

Clips are at least 1080x1920: by default the output is the largest frame of the crop's aspect that fits 1080x1920 (1920x1080 for a landscape aspect), or the crop window's own size when that is bigger, so 9:16 of a 720p or 1080p source is 1080x1920, 1:1 is 1080x1080 and 9:16 of 4K keeps 1214x2160. Small sources are scaled up with high-quality smoothing, and the captions, end card, stamp and watermark are painted at the output's size, so their text stays sharp full screen on a phone instead of being drawn at 404x720 and magnified. `crop.height` (and the picker's `height` default) overrides it in either direction. An H.264 encoder that takes frames and never outputs or errors (macOS's hardware encoder under load) no longer hangs `createClip`: after `stallTimeout` seconds (default 15) without output while reel waits on it, the encoder is closed, the clip is tried once more with a software encoder where the browser has one, and otherwise it rejects with a `ClipError` whose reason is `'encoder-stalled'` (new type `ClipErrorReason`). The picker then says "The export stalled. Try again." (`labels.exportStalled`, the button reading `labels.retry`), fires a fatal `reel-error` with that reason and goes back to editing; `stallTimeout` is also a picker default.

Captions burned into the picture: `captions: { cues, style }` takes parsed cues, WebVTT text, a `TextTrack`, or the URL of a WebVTT file (a `URL` or a string that is not caption text), fetched with the clip's signal; captions are WebVTT only, so convert SRT to WebVTT first. `captions.tracks` takes several tracks in video-player's `tracks` shape and burns in the one marked `default`, else the one matching `navigator.language`, else the first. Captions have one look: white bold text on a translucent dark box behind each line, 4.5% of the frame's height, 14% above the bottom, wrapping at 86% of the width onto as many lines as a cue needs, made for desktop and TV viewing. `style` is a `CaptionStyle` of overrides on it: `fontFamily`, `fontWeight`, `size` (a fraction of the height), `color`, `background` (or `null` for no box), `position` (`'top'`, `'middle'` or `'bottom'`), `margin` and `maxWidth`; a host posting to TikTok or Reels can raise the captions with `position` and `margin`. Captions that will not load never fail a clip: a failed fetch (an HTTP error, CORS) or a file that is not WebVTT (no `WEBVTT` header) leaves the clip without captions and calls `onWarning({ reason: 'captions-unavailable', target: 'captions', message })` (`console.warn` by default); `loadCaptions`, called directly, rejects with an `Error` saying why. New exports `parseVtt`, `isCaptionText`, `toCues` and `loadCaptions`.

Clips point back at their moment: `clipLink(origin, start, end)` is the origin URL with `#ml-t=start,end` and, with `origin.player`, `&ml-player=<id>` (the one link `@munsonlabs/video-player`'s deep links answer), and with an `origin` a clip's metadata carries the title, the publisher, that deep link and an encoder tag (MP4 atoms); `metadata: false` leaves them out. A source's own tags are never copied.

End cards, a logo stamp and a watermark. `endCard` adds a silent card after the clip, faded in over its last frame, built for phone viewers: logo, publisher, headline and the article's readable address (`acme.news/2026/10/tides-rising`, from `origin.url` or `endCard.displayUrl`, shortened in the middle to fit), all defaulting from `origin`, themeable, or drawn by your own `draw` (`drawEndCard` is exported to add to it). `stamp` draws a logo over every clip frame, placed clear of TikTok/Reels/Shorts UI by default, and `watermark` runs a thin strip of text over it. Logos from a URL load with CORS and SVG support, and a logo that cannot be used is left out with an `onWarning` (`'logo-unavailable'`) instead of failing the clip. New exports `readableUrl` and `planStamp`. There is no QR code: most clips are watched on phones, which cannot scan their own screen.

`createStoryboard()` builds a JPEG sprite of thumbnails and a WebVTT storyboard (`#xywh=`) for scrubber previews, over a `start`/`end` range: by default each thumbnail is the nearest keyframe at or before its time, each keyframe decoded once (`exact: true` decodes exact frames), each thumbnail is reported through `onTile` as it is drawn, and `signal` stops it at once.

HLS that works like a file. Variants are chosen from the master playlist's `RESOLUTION`, `BANDWIDTH` and `CODECS`: `canClip` reads playlists only (`info` describes the largest decodable variant, `info.videoTracks` lists them all), storyboards read the smallest variant that fills a tile, and `createClip` reads the smallest that covers the output after the crop, with that variant's own audio. A `track` option (`'auto' | 'smallest' | 'largest' | (tracks) => track`) overrides the choice. `listCaptionTracks(source, { tracks })` and `loadCaptionTrack(source, id, { start, end, tracks })` read the `tracks` passed in (as `passed:<n>`), HLS `SUBTITLES` renditions (only the WebVTT segments a range overlaps, `X-TIMESTAMP-MAP` honoured) and a `<video>`'s text tracks (a disabled one is read and restored); `captions: { track: id }` burns one in, and `captions.cues` still wins; a rendition whose playlist or segments fail is left out with the same `'captions-unavailable'` warning, and `loadCaptionTrack`, called directly, rejects with an `Error`. `createPlaylistCache()`: pass it as `cache` to `canClip`, `createStoryboard`, `createClip`, `listCaptionTracks` and `loadCaptionTrack` and each HLS playlist is fetched once, whole, Mediabunny's range reads included (live media playlists are still re-read).

The picker, inline on the page's player. `ReelPicker` (`@munsonlabs/reel/vue`) and `<ml-reel-picker>` (`@munsonlabs/reel/element`) hook into the page's `@munsonlabs/video-player`, which is the preview: opening pauses it on the moment, loops the range with `setClipRange()` and draws a 9:16 crop window over its picture (drag or arrow keys to reframe, the stamp and the captions previewed inside it), while the filmstrip with its handles and playhead, the end-card and logo toggles, export with progress and cancel, and the finished clip (Share, Download, Copy caption with link, Edit again) sit in a panel wherever the page places it. The picker finds its player from `player` (the handle), `for` (an `<ml-video-player>`'s id) or the enclosing `VideoPlayer`; `source`/`src` defaults to the player's file, and a player on a `blob:` MediaSource (HLS through hls.js) needs the stream's URL as `source`. While it is open the player's HUD is hidden (`setControls(false)`). The captions burned in are the track the viewer has on in the player, read from its `<video>` or matched to the HLS rendition. The panel carries the player's own controls (play and pause, the time within the clip, mute, and a captions button that cycles its tracks) and, when a track is on, a Caption position control (top, middle or bottom, `captionPosition` on the picker) that moves the captions in the window and the clip alike; the browser's own rendering of the captions is hidden under the window, so only the clip's show. A picker without a player is blocked as `'no-player'`, and one with nothing it can read as `'no-source'`. Reel's core and Mediabunny load on first open; the page-wide labels, limits, logo and share caption are set with `setPickerDefaults()`.

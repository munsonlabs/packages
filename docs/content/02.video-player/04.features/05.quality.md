Quality selection needs real variant streams, so it applies to adaptive HLS and DASH on the native `<video>` path. YouTube, Vimeo, and Dailymotion expose no quality override - they manage it themselves inside their iframe. Brightcove and JW Player get it wherever their resolved source is adaptive. Plain MP4 has no variants, so no Quality menu.

## Adaptive HLS / DASH

A Quality option appears on its own once hls.js or dash.js has parsed the manifest's resolution variants - there's no prop:

## Selecting a level

The control cycles Auto → highest → … → lowest → Auto. **Auto leaves the streaming engine's adaptive bitrate algorithm in control**, which is the right default for most viewers - picking a level pins it and disables ABR until you restore Auto.

Programmatically, `setQuality(index)` selects a level and `null` restores Auto, firing `qualitychange` - on your own calls and on the engine's own ABR switches alike. Levels are exposed as `{ index, height, bitrate, label }`.

The list is one rung per resolution, ascending, not one per manifest variant. A ladder usually carries each resolution several times over at different bitrates and codecs - bipbop above lists 1080p nine times - so each height is represented by its highest-bitrate variant, and audio-only renditions are left out. `index` stays the engine's own, so it's still what `setQuality` takes.

::warning
Safari plays HLS natively, and native HLS offers no manual quality-override hook - so the Quality menu won't appear there, while [captions](/video-player/features/captions) still work.
::

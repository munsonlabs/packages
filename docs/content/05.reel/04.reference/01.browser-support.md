Reel needs WebCodecs (`VideoEncoder`, `VideoDecoder`), `OffscreenCanvas` and a secure context. Every number here comes from the real-browser suite running through Playwright on an Apple M3 Pro (macOS 26.7): Chromium 151, WebKit 26.5 and Firefox 153, all passing.

## Encoders

Clips are always MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio, so those are the two encoders that matter.

| `isConfigSupported`              | Chromium | WebKit | Firefox |
| -------------------------------- | -------- | ------ | ------- |
| H.264 `avc1.640028` at 1080x1920 | yes      | yes    | yes     |
| AAC `mp4a.40.2`                  | yes      | yes    | **no**  |

`canClip` checks the H.264 encoder at the clip's size in the viewer's browser, and `canClip(...).plan.audio` says what happens to the sound.

## Output by engine

| Source                     | Chromium                      | WebKit           | Firefox                                              |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| AAC audio (most MP4s, HLS) | MP4, AAC copied               | MP4, AAC copied  | MP4, **AAC copied** (no encoder needed)              |
| Opus or other audio        | MP4, AAC encoded              | MP4, AAC encoded | MP4, **silent**, with an `audio-unavailable` warning |
| Rotated phone video        | upright, no rotation metadata | same             | same                                                 |

AAC audio is copied packet for packet, which is what keeps sound in Firefox clips of AAC sources. Firefox has no AAC encoder, so a source with other audio (a WebM with Opus, say) gives a silent clip there, and `onWarning` (in the picker, a note on the finished clip) says so.

## Speed

10 seconds cropped to 9:16 with captions, one engine at a time, on an Apple-silicon Mac (time, then file size):

| Source, output                        | Chromium     | WebKit       | Firefox       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------- |
| 720p, 404x720 (`crop.height: 720`)    | ~0.9s, 1.3MB | ~1.3s, 0.7MB | ~0.6s, 2.0MB  |
| 720p, 1080x1920 (the default)         | ~1.8s, 6.8MB | ~1.8s, 3.4MB | ~1.6s, 7.9MB  |
| 1080p, 608x1080 (`crop.height: 1080`) | ~1.0s, 2.5MB | ~2.1s, 1.3MB | ~0.9s, 3.3MB  |
| 1080p, 1080x1920 (the default)        | ~1.9s, 7.1MB | ~2.6s, 3.4MB | ~1.8s, 10.3MB |

The default 1080x1920 costs about twice the time and three to five times the bytes of the crop's own size, and buys captions, the end card and the stamp drawn at the size a phone shows them (see [crop](/reel/making-clips/crop#output-size)). Faster than real time in every engine either way; the tests never assert speed, only output.

## A stalled encoder

macOS's hardware H.264 encoder, when busy, can take frames and never output or error. `createClip` watches for that: if the encoder holds frames without producing anything for `stallTimeout` seconds (15 by default) while reel waits on it, the attempt is abandoned, the encoder closed, and the clip tried once more with a software encoder (`hardwareAcceleration: 'prefer-software'`) where the browser has one at that size (Chromium, WebKit and Firefox all do on macOS). If there is none, or that stalls too, it rejects with a `ClipError` whose reason is `'encoder-stalled'`; trying again usually works. The picker shows "The export stalled. Try again." and fires `reel-error`.

## HLS

Reel reads HLS itself, in every engine, through Mediabunny: the master playlist, then only the variant and segments it needs. Variants are chosen from the playlist's `RESOLUTION`, `BANDWIDTH` and `CODECS`, so checking a stream (`canClip`) downloads playlists only, and a variant this browser cannot decode is never picked.

| Job                       | Variant read                                                     |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `canClip`                 | None: playlists only                                             |
| Storyboard / filmstrip    | The smallest at least as big as a thumbnail (usually the lowest) |
| `createClip`              | The smallest that covers the output after the crop               |
| Caption timing (`MPEGTS`) | The first segment of the lowest variant, once                    |

On Mux's five-variant Big Buck Bunny (240p to 1080p, 10s MPEG-TS segments), the picker's filmstrip went from a blank strip after 25 to 30 seconds and about 95 MB of 1080p segment ranges (all 5 variants probed) to its first thumbnail in under a second and all ten in about 2.4 seconds, from 15 requests and about 2.8 MB of the 240p variant (Chromium 151, same network). Opening the page's `canClip` went from 16 requests and 7.6 MB to 2 requests and 5 KB.

The **picker has no player of its own**: the page's `@munsonlabs/video-player` is the preview, so HLS plays as that player plays it, natively in Safari and through hls.js (its optional peer) elsewhere. The filmstrip and the export never go through the player; they read the stream with Mediabunny.

In the test suite, Playwright's WebKit loads the ladder fixture's MPEG-TS segments natively but never advances its clock, so the preview's playback tests use the fMP4 stream there; the ladder's subtitles are tested without playing it. Safari plays MPEG-TS HLS.

## Workarounds

**WebKit crop.** `drawImage(VideoFrame, sx, sy, sw, sh, …)` ignores the source rectangle in WebKit and draws the whole frame, so any crop done with it (including Mediabunny's own `crop` option) comes out uncropped and squashed in Safari. Reel draws the whole frame offset and scaled instead, which is correct everywhere. A dedicated test pins the WebKit behaviour.

**Firefox AAC.** No `AudioEncoder` for AAC, so AAC sources are copied rather than re-encoded, and other sources are clipped without sound (`audio-unavailable`).

**WebKit and detached media elements.** In the test suite, a `<video>` created only to check that a clip plays, and referenced by nothing but its own listeners, occasionally fired neither `loadeddata` nor `error` in WebKit under load. The test helper now holds the element and times out with its state; reel itself never relies on such an element.
